Filing a Complaint
To report a cybercrime, you go to the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in) and
click “File a Complaint.” The portal will ask you to accept terms, then guide you through a step-by-step
form. First you must log in: enter your name and mobile number, click “Get OTP,” and verify the onetime code sent to your phone . Once logged in, you pick a Category and Sub-Category for your
complaint (e.g. online fraud, hacking, social-media crime, etc.) . Next you fill in incident details
(date/time, description, where it happened, evidence like screenshots, etc.) and (if known) suspect
details. You then provide your own details (the complainant) – gender, age, address, email, and upload
an ID proof (voter ID, passport, etc.) . Finally you preview your entry and click “Submit.”
After submission the portal immediately displays a Complaint ID and shows a confirmation message
. You also automatically get an SMS and email with this ID . The portal lets you download your
submitted complaint as a PDF for your records . In short, the portal walks you through: (1) accept the
terms and start a “Report Other Cyber Crime” complaint ; (2) log in with mobile+OTP ; (3) select
crime category ; (4) enter incident and suspect details; (5) enter your own details; and (6) submit and
note the generated Complaint ID .
Privacy and Anonymity
You log in with your personal mobile number so the portal knows who you are. (This is by design – it
uses OTP verification for security .) For most crimes, your identity and contact info are recorded so
that the police can follow up with you. Importantly, the portal provides an anonymous reporting option
– but only for very sensitive cases. If you are reporting child pornography or sexual assault content (CP/
Rape material), you can choose the “Report Anonymously” workflow . In that mode your name/ID
aren’t collected, only the crime details. For all other categories, however, you must register with a
mobile number and (optionally) email. This means your data (name, mobile, etc.) goes to the police
along with the complaint. The portal runs on a secure government server (NIC) and uses OTP/login to
protect your account .
Case Assignment & Handling
Once you submit a report, the portal automatically routes it to the appropriate police agency. In
practice, the user manual explains that complaints are sent to the State/UT law enforcement agency
based on the address of the complainant . In other words, your case goes to your local cyber
police or jurisdiction. This portal is not itself the police – it’s a reporting channel. Official guidelines
stress that a portal complaint is not an FIR; it simply “facilitates public reporting” and “should not be
treated as an FIR” . The state/UT police who receive the case decide whether to register a formal FIR
and investigate further. A government press release confirms: “incidents reported on this portal, [and]
their conversion into FIRs and subsequent action thereon are handled by the LEAs of State/UT
concerned” . In summary, the portal logs your complaint, assigns it by region, and hands it off to
local police for any follow-up. (There is no built-in “score” or priority tag in the portal itself – all
prioritization is up to the investigating officers.)

Tracking and Updates
After filing, you can track your complaint’s progress on the portal. Log back in with your user name
and mobile (entering a fresh OTP), then go to “Check Status” and select the date or complaint ID .
The portal will display any updates entered by the police. In practice, every time the authorities take a
step (like converting the report into an FIR or making an update), they push a status update to the
portal. Your registered mobile and email are also notified of these updates . In short, once your
complaint is on the portal you’ll see a running status: you get the unique Complaint ID as proof of filing,
and then any action taken by police will be shown in your account and sent to your phone/email
.
Escalation and Other Options
If you feel your case isn’t being handled properly by the local agency, the portal advises contacting the
Grievance Officer of your State/UT . Every state police department has a designated cybercrime
grievance officer you can appeal to. In addition, the portal offers a couple of extra features: you can
withdraw a reported complaint (as long as an FIR hasn’t been filed yet) by logging in, choosing “Case
Withdrawal,” and submitting a reason . After withdrawal, the complaint is marked canceled in the
system. (Once a police FIR is registered, you can no longer withdraw it via the portal .) The portal
also lets you recover your username or update your mobile number if needed (but those are minor
account-management tools).
Sources: The above steps and processes are documented in the official User Manual and government
releases for India’s National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal . Each element – from
OTP login and category selection to assignment by state police and status tracking – is described in
those sources. These describe the exact flow of online reporting, how the portal preserves anonymity
for sensitive cases, and how investigators are notified.
